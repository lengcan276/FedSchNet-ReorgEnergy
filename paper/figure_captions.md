## Figure captions

### Figure 1.  Task-client heterogeneity across the five-node organic-electronics federation.

**(a)** Representative molecules and per-node summary statistics for the five task-client nodes — A (QM9 non-aromatic public, **cation reorganization energy**, n = 6,020); B (QM9 aromatic public, **cation reorganization energy**, n = 9,190); C-hole (in-house TADF, **hole reorganization energy**, n = 53); C-triplet (in-house TADF, **triplet reorganization energy**, n = 49); D (Atahan-Evrenk 2019, **hole reorganization energy**, n = 5,876).  All five quantities are reorganization-energy-related but they are *not* a single regression target: A and B are cation-λ, C-hole and D are hole-λ but at different DFT functionals and different chemical spaces, and C-triplet is a triplet-λ task.  Each representative is the molecule whose label is closest to the per-node median, chosen by a deterministic rule prior to any model training; no representatives were selected by hand.  The bar plot on the right reports the per-node label mean (eV) with error bars showing one standard deviation.  **(b)** Per-node label distributions on a common eV axis (jittered to reveal density).  The label means span an order of magnitude (0.26–2.64 eV) and the supporting DFT protocols differ across nodes (Table 1).  The figure motivates the task-client abstraction adopted in this work — different physical quantities measured at the same laboratory are not interchangeable regression targets, regardless of how chemically similar the underlying molecules are.  Figure 1 is descriptive: no statistical test is reported here.

### Figure 2.  PC²-FedReorg architecture and per-key aggregation routing.

Schematic summary of the federated training pipeline.  Each of the five task-client lanes (A, B, C-hole, C-triplet, D) instantiates the same logical model: input molecule → shared encoder → optional residual adapter → **quantity-specific regression head (always private)** → **private (μ, σ) calibration buffer** → predicted λ in eV-space.  The top band depicts the federation server's per-key aggregation routing: encoder parameters are aggregated under T_repr, adapter parameters under T_adapter, head parameters under T_head, and calibration buffers are never aggregated.  The bottom band summarizes the auditable compatibility metadata — quantity, DFT protocol, label-scale comparability, and chemical-space overlap — that determines the three transferability matrices (Section 2.4).  Figure 2 is **schematic**; it visualises the algorithm and is not a performance claim.  The performance-supported mechanism in the present study is the task-specific calibration buffer (§4.4); the transferability gate is shown here to make the routing logic auditable, not to claim a performance contribution from the gate itself.

### Figure 3.  Auditable transferability matrices T_repr, T_adapter, T_head.

Heat maps of the three 5 × 5 transferability matrices used by PC²-FedReorg's per-key aggregation routing.  Rows index the source task-client *i*; columns index the target task-client *j*.  **T_repr** (left) is driven by chemistry overlap, computed as a weighted sum of mean max-Tanimoto similarity *K* (Morgan radius 2, 2048 bits) and asymmetric Murcko-scaffold overlap *S*.  **T_adapter** (middle) is the geometric mean √(T_repr · T_head) with a small floor.  **T_head** (right) applies the conservative clauses described in Section 2.4 (quantity mismatch, V1 hard rule, unknown protocol fields, functional mismatch, plus a same-source shortcut for clients derived from a single public dataset).  The only non-zero off-diagonal in T_head is **A ↔ B** (= 0.33), which is permitted by the same-source shortcut because both clients are split from a single QM9 cation-reorganization-energy source; this is the only ordered pair in our federation for which a public head-sharing channel is opened.  The highlighted invariants T_head[D, C-hole] = T_head[D, C-triplet] = T_head[C-hole, C-triplet] = T_head[C-triplet, C-hole] = 0 follow deterministically from the protocol metadata and quantity definitions; the `reasons` field of `T_transferability.json` reports the first clause that fired in each case.  The transferability matrices are released in `results/preverify/T_transferability.json` and can be audited prior to any aggregation round.  Figure 3 documents the algorithm's compatibility decisions; it is not claimed as a source of performance improvement.  Whether the gate also affects performance is examined empirically in Figure 5.

### Figure 4.  Main performance comparison on the two private TADF tasks.

Mean absolute error (eV) for five federated methods evaluated by 5-seed leave-one-out cross-validation on **(left)** C-hole (n = 53) and **(right)** C-triplet (n = 49).  Bars show the per-method mean MAE across five seeds; error bars show the standard error of the mean.  Significance brackets compare PC²-FedReorg (rightmost bar in each panel) with FedAvg, FedProx and FedPer using **paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests** on per-molecule absolute errors (medians across seeds); reported p-values are unadjusted and no multiple-comparison correction is applied (see SI for the full paired table).  **On C-triplet**, PC²-FedReorg achieves MAE = 0.660 eV versus 0.762–0.784 eV for the three baselines, with paired Wilcoxon p = 0.045, 0.045 and 0.042 against FedAvg, FedProx and FedPer respectively.  We label these comparisons as **nominally significant** rather than significant: the p-values sit just below the conventional 0.05 threshold, are unadjusted, and the bootstrap 95 % confidence interval on the PC²-vs-FedAvg paired MAE difference touches zero at its upper bound (Table 3).  **On C-hole**, all evaluated methods produce MAE within the narrow band 0.368–0.414 eV; every paired comparison returns p ≥ 0.20 after rounding.  We therefore report PC²-FedReorg as nominally significant on C-triplet but statistically indistinguishable on C-hole.  The C-hole non-significance is consistent with the small-sample data ceiling identified by classical baselines (Supporting Information Section S2).  *The targeted E71 calibration-only control that decomposes this gain is analyzed in Figure 5.*

### Figure 5.  Calibration under personalized heads explains the C-triplet gain.

Three-panel decomposition relating PC²-FedReorg's nominally significant C-triplet improvement reported in Figure 4 to a targeted control experiment (E71).  **Panel (a)** plots per-method **5-seed mean MAE ± SEM** on C-triplet (dark bars) and C-hole (light bars) for four configurations: **E63** FedPer (no calibration), **E70** PC²-FedReorg without calibration, **E66** the full PC²-FedReorg architecture, and **E71** a FedPer baseline endowed only with the per-task-client (μ, σ) calibration buffer (no residual adapter, no transferability-driven routing).  On C-triplet, E71 (0.653 ± 0.011 eV) and E66 (0.671 ± 0.010 eV) sit at the same level while E63 (0.755 ± 0.007 eV) and E70 (0.786 ± 0.006 eV) sit above; on C-hole all four configurations cluster in the 0.368–0.384 eV band, with no method separation detected at the present sample size (Figure 4 left).  *Reporting convention.* Panel (a) shows the 5-seed mean of per-method MAE_mean (matching Table 2); paired comparisons in panel (b) use the per-molecule median-across-seeds absolute error vector required for paired Wilcoxon testing (matching Table 3), so the reference and comparison MAEs reported below differ from panel (a) by ≤ 0.003 eV (below seed-SEM).  **Panel (b)** is a forest plot of paired ΔMAE on C-triplet for three E71 contrasts (top three rows; **MAE(E71) − MAE(other)**, negative ⇒ E71 has lower paired MAE) and the four planned PC² component ablations (rows 4–7; **MAE(PC²) − MAE(other)**, negative ⇒ PC² has lower paired MAE).  Error bars are bootstrap 95 % CIs (5,000 resamples of molecule indices); the dashed vertical line marks zero.  The three E71 contrasts: **E71 vs. PC²-FedReorg (E66): ΔMAE = −0.006 eV (95 % CI (−0.044, +0.029); paired Wilcoxon p = 0.929, n.s.); E71 vs. FedPer (E63): ΔMAE = −0.109 eV (95 % CI (−0.208, −0.010); p = 0.028 nominal); E71 vs. PC² without calibration (E70): ΔMAE = −0.125 eV (95 % CI (−0.224, −0.027); p = 0.021 nominal)**.  Of the four PC² ablations, only **E70 (removing calibration) on C-triplet** crosses the nominal 0.05 threshold (paired Wilcoxon p = 0.043) with a CI entirely below zero — identifying task-specific calibration as the only component whose presence has detectable ablation support.  **E69 (uniform gate) is numerically slightly better than PC²-FedReorg on C-triplet (ΔMAE = +0.043 eV in favor of E69) but statistically indistinguishable** (p = 0.18; CI crosses zero), and **E68 (no C-cross representation share) shows the same direction with p = 0.07**.  **Panel (c)** summarises the reading: FedPer + calibration alone (E71) is sufficient to reproduce PC²-FedReorg's observed C-triplet performance at our sample size; no additional benefit from the chemistry/protocol gate is detected; C-hole shows no method separation across all four configurations.  All p-values are paired Wilcoxon nominal values on 5-seed-median per-molecule absolute errors; the Bonferroni threshold for the three E71 contrasts is α/3 ≈ 0.017 and neither of the two effect-bearing E71 comparisons crosses it, so the report combines the p-value, the bootstrap CI direction and the per-molecule sign count (see §4.4).  Raw E71 outputs: `results/e71_summary.csv`, `results/e71_predictions.json`, `results/e71_vs_*.{csv,md}`.

Three molecules in the C-triplet test set where PC²-FedReorg achieves the largest reduction in absolute error relative to FedAvg (left column, green outlines) and three where it shows the largest degradation (right column, red outlines), selected by the per-molecule ΔMAE = |error|_PC² − |error|_FedAvg.  Per-molecule absolute errors are aggregated as the median across the five seeds. ## Table captions

### Table 1.  Task-client dataset summary.

Per-node summary of the five task-client nodes used in this study.  Columns: dataset source, physical quantity measured, sample count after preprocessing, per-node label mean ± standard deviation (eV), per-node label range (eV), and the DFT protocol fields populated in `protocol_meta` (functional, basis, charge state, geometry treatment).  Sample counts come from `results/preverify/T_transferability.json:n_kept`.  Unknown protocol fields for D (geometry, charge state, conformer policy) trigger the conservative T_head clauses described in Section 2.4.  The "quantity" column distinguishes cation reorganization energy (A, B), hole reorganization energy (C-hole, D), and triplet reorganization energy (C-triplet) — a distinction that the *physical-client* abstraction would conflate but that PC²-FedReorg's *task-client* abstraction preserves.

### Table 2.  Main performance summary — 5-seed mean and SEM.

Mean absolute error (eV), root-mean-squared error (eV), and coefficient of determination *R*² for five federated methods (Local-only, FedAvg, FedProx, FedPer, PC²-FedReorg) on four evaluation cells: Client A 5-fold cross-validation, Client B 5-fold cross-validation, Client C-hole leave-one-out cross-validation (n = 53), and Client C-triplet leave-one-out cross-validation (n = 49).  All values are means across five random seeds {42, 123, 456, 789, 1000}; the accompanying SEM column reports the standard error of the seed-level mean.  MAE and RMSE are the primary regression metrics; *R*² is reported as auxiliary because small-sample LOOCV is known to produce negative *R*² even for well-calibrated predictors (best classical baseline *R*² = −0.10 on C-hole, +0.08 on C-triplet; Supporting Information Section S2).  Full per-seed values are tabulated in Supporting Information Section S5.

### Table 3.  Paired statistical tests on C-hole and C-triplet.

For each pair (PC²-FedReorg, comparator) and for each LOOCV target, we report sample size, per-method MAE, paired MAE difference (PC² − comparator), bootstrap 95 % confidence interval on the difference (5,000 resamples of molecule indices), per-molecule win / loss counts, the two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value, and a significance flag (* p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001).  Per-molecule absolute errors are the median across the five seeds, yielding a deterministic 53- or 49-element per-method error vector.  Table 3 is the primary statistical evidence base for the manuscript: it supports the C-triplet significance claim (p < 0.05 versus FedAvg, FedProx and FedPer) and the C-hole non-significance claim (every paired p ≥ 0.20 after rounding, with bootstrap CIs straddling zero).

### Table 4.  Ablation summary.

Per-method MAE (eV, mean ± SEM across five seeds) for PC²-FedReorg (reference, E66) and four targeted ablations: **E70** — task-specific calibration removed; **E68** — chemistry-driven cross-quantity encoder sharing removed (T_repr cross terms floored to ε); **E69** — uniform FedAvg weighting in place of the chemistry/protocol gate (calibration and adapter retained); **E65** — D → C supervised pretrain followed by C fine-tune (negative-transfer control).  All ablations were trained with the same hyperparameters, seeds and code path as PC²-FedReorg; the differences are confined to the indicated component.  E70 is the only ablation that produces a statistically significant degradation relative to PC²-FedReorg (C-triplet paired Wilcoxon p = 0.043; see Table 3); we therefore conclude that task-specific calibration is the only ablation-supported active ingredient in the present study.  E68 and E69 leave performance statistically unchanged, indicating that chemistry-driven gate weighting is not the source of PC²-FedReorg's C-triplet improvement and is positioned in this paper as auditable compatibility metadata rather than a performance lever.
